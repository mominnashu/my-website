Already clicked a link or paid? Do this now

Act fast. The first hours matter most.

  1. Call your bank's official helpline right away and ask them to block your card, UPI and net banking. Use the number on your card or the bank's official website, never a number from the message.
  2. Call 1930, India's cyber fraud helpline, as soon as you can. Reporting quickly improves the chance of stopping the money.
  3. Report it at cybercrime.gov.in. Keep screenshots, the sender's number, links, transaction IDs and the time.
  4. Change the passwords and PINs for your bank, email and the app you used, and turn on two-step verification.
  5. If you installed an app such as AnyDesk, uninstall it. If you shared an OTP, tell your bank immediately.
  6. Do not pay anyone who offers to get your money back. That is a second scam.

Questions, answered

How does the check work?

It uses rules for common scam patterns: urgent threats, requests for OTPs or PINs, fake KYC notices, prize offers and fake job tasks. Every link is compared with official brand domains to catch look-alikes, shortened links and risky endings.

What does the AI check do?

Right after each scan, Claude reads the message or link address, judges how likely it is to be a scam, and explains the red flags in plain words. It uses your own Claude account, and if you decline the permission you still get the rule-based result. The AI cannot open links, so it can still be wrong: treat it as a second opinion, not proof.

Does it open the link?

No. It reads only the address and the wording. It cannot see the live page, so a new scam site with a clean-looking address can slip through.

Is it free? Do I need an account?

It is free and needs no account. Paste a message or link and press scan.

It says dangerous. What should I do?

Do not click, reply or enter any password, OTP, PIN or card number. Block the sender. If you already paid or shared details, call 1930 or report at cybercrime.gov.in quickly.

A real company was flagged. Why?

Rules can give false alarms. Check the sender through the company's official app or website. Never trust a number or link given inside the message itself.

Which brands are checked?

A link that uses one of these names but is not on its official domain is flagged. Brands can change domains, so verify them yourself.
